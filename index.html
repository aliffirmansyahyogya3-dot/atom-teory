    Discoveries behind the models

    Dalton's atomic theory

    Careful measurement of reacting masses showed elements combine in fixed, simple ratios — evidence for discrete atoms.

    Thomson's cathode rays

    Rays in a vacuum tube bent toward a positive plate, revealing a light, negatively charged particle inside the atom.

    Rutherford's gold foil

    Firing particles at thin gold foil, most passed through — but a few bounced straight back, revealing a dense nucleus.

    Bohr's hydrogen spectrum

    Hydrogen gas emits light only at specific colors, matching electrons jumping between fixed energy levels.

    Schrödinger's wave mechanics

    Treating electrons as waves produced equations that predicted atomic behavior far more accurately than fixed orbits.

    Try it: the gold foil experiment

    Fire particles at a thin sheet of gold foil. Watch how most pass straight through, some deflect slightly, and a rare few bounce almost straight back — the evidence for a tiny, dense nucleus.

    About this journey

    Each model on this page was the best available explanation of the atom in its time. None were wrong for their era — each was replaced because new evidence demanded a better explanation. That process of revision is the story of science itself.
